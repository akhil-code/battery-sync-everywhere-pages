Customize Menu Bar…

Mac menu bar

The device that needs charging comes first.

Normally the menu bar shows your Mac’s own battery. When another device runs low, it takes that spot while it’s low. Open the popover to see everything, with low devices at the top.

Or pick up to four devices to keep in the menu bar, shown with battery or device icons.

  • ⌘R checks iCloud for newer readings
  • ⌘P pauses reporting from this Mac
  • ⌘D opens the full dashboard

iPhone and iPad widgets

Your Mac’s battery on your Home Screen.

Small, medium and large widgets show your other devices, styled like the built-in Batteries widget. On the Lock Screen, Battery Sync shows another device, because this one’s level is already in the status bar.

When a widget refreshes, it also uploads this device’s level, so your other devices get one more update.

Honest numbers

Every reading shows how old it is.

A battery level is only useful if you know when it was measured. Battery Sync never estimates or fills gaps. It shows the last real reading and its age, and greys it out once it’s more than an hour old.

Check for Newer Reading

Ask an iPhone or iPad to report right now.

If a reading looks old, ask for a new one from your Mac or another device. The request goes through your own iCloud, and the iPhone or iPad answers in the background when iOS allows it.

  1. Request. Your Mac saves a request to your private iCloud.
  2. Wake. iCloud sends a silent push to that device. You can switch on a visible notification if you want to see each check.
  3. Report. The device measures its battery and uploads it. Your Mac waits up to 60 seconds.

iOS decides when background work runs, so a check can’t be guaranteed. If nothing arrives, you keep the last reading and its time. Opening Battery Sync on the device always takes a fresh one.

Sharing

Keep an eye on the family’s batteries too.

Share your devices with someone on a different Apple Account. Invite them, or send a link. Your devices then show up on theirs, under your name, in the app, the widgets, the Mac menu bar and low-battery alerts.

It works both ways, so everyone in a household can share and see every device from one place.

View only
People who join see your devices’ names, battery levels, charging state and history. They can’t rename, remove or change anything.
Readings on request, if you allow it
Turn on Can request readings for one person, and they can ask your iPhones and iPads for a fresh reading, the same way you do.
Undo it any time
Remove one person or stop sharing with everyone. On their side, Stop Viewing takes your devices off all of theirs.

Low-battery alerts

Find out before it dies in a drawer.

Choose a threshold and Battery Sync alerts you once when another device drops below it. The alert resets after the device charges. If a low reading arrives late, the alert says so and how long the device was offline.

Privacy

It stays in your iCloud.

Battery Sync has no server and no account of its own. Readings go to the private iCloud database of your Apple Account. Only your devices can read them, plus anyone you choose to share with. The developer can’t see them.

Read the privacy policy

  • No location or serial numbersJust battery level, charging state, device name, times and an install ID.

When each device reports

Apple sets different rules for each platform, so here’s exactly what to expect.

Mac

Reports when power changes, when it wakes, and every 10, 15 or 30 minutes while awake. It never makes up readings for time it was asleep.

iPhone and iPad

Report when the app is open, when iOS allows a background refresh, when the widget updates, and when you ask with Check for Newer Reading.

On every charge

On iPhone and iPad, add the Report Battery action to a Shortcuts automation that runs when you connect or disconnect a charger. Setup in the app walks you through it.
