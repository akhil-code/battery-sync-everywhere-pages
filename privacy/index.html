Battery Sync

Privacy Policy

Effective November 21, 2026

Battery Sync shows the battery levels of your iPhone, iPad and Mac in one place. This policy explains what the app stores, where it is stored, and how you can delete it. It applies to the Battery Sync app, its widgets and its notification extension on iPhone, iPad and Mac.

In short

  • Your battery readings are stored in your own private iCloud database. The developer cannot see them.
  • Battery Sync has no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no tracking.
  • The app does not collect your location, contacts or hardware serial numbers.
  • You can delete a device’s history, or all Battery Sync data, from the app at any time.

Data stored in your iCloud

To show your devices’ batteries on each other, Battery Sync saves the following to the private CloudKit database of the Apple Account you are signed in to:

  • Battery level and charging state.
  • Device name, device type (iPhone, iPad or Mac) and an identifier the app creates for each installation.
  • When each reading was measured and synced.
  • Basic context for each reading, such as what triggered it (for example the app, a widget refresh or a requested check).
  • When you ask an iPhone or iPad for a fresh reading, the request, its time and the installation identifiers of the two devices involved.

This private database belongs to you and is managed by Apple under the Apple Privacy Policy. Only devices signed in to the same Apple Account can read it. The developer of Battery Sync has no access to it.

Data kept only on your device

  • Diagnostic log. Each device keeps a local log of readings and sync attempts, including app and OS versions, whether the app was in the foreground or background, Low Power Mode, Background App Refresh status and sync errors. It is kept for 10 days and never leaves the device unless you export it.
  • Settings. Preferences such as device names, pinned devices, low-battery alert thresholds and menu bar choices are stored locally.
  • Widget data. The app shares the latest readings with its widgets through storage that only Battery Sync can read.

Notifications

When one device asks another for a fresh reading, iCloud sends a push notification to the target device through Apple Push Notification service. These pushes are silent by default. If you turn on report notifications, the target device shows an alert, reads its battery and updates the alert with the result. Low-battery alerts are created on your device from readings already in your iCloud. Battery Sync does not send your push token to any server of its own.

What Battery Sync does not do

  • It does not collect location, contacts, photos, hardware serial numbers or advertising identifiers.
  • It does not use analytics, advertising or third-party tracking SDKs.
  • It does not sell or share your data, and it does not track you across apps or websites.
  • It does not run its own server. Sync and notifications use Apple’s iCloud and push services only.

Exports you choose to make

You can export a device’s readings as a CSV file. The file is created only when you ask and goes wherever you choose to save or share it. After that, it is handled by the app or service you send it to.

Deleting your data

  • Remove this device and delete history (in Settings) stops reporting from that device, deletes its readings from your iCloud and clears its local queue and diagnostic log.
  • Delete account deletes Battery Sync data for every device in your iCloud. Your Apple Account itself is not affected.
  • Pause reporting stops new readings from being uploaded without deleting existing ones.
  • Deleting the app removes its local data, but readings already in iCloud stay until you delete them with one of the options above.

Earlier test builds

Some early test versions of Battery Sync registered a device’s push token and installation identifier with a small server operated by the developer to deliver wake requests. That service has been retired and no longer accepts data. Current versions do not contact it.

Children

Battery Sync is a general-audience utility. It does not knowingly collect personal information from children, and the data it stores stays in the user’s own iCloud.

Changes to this policy

If Battery Sync changes how it handles data, this page will be updated and the effective date above will change.
